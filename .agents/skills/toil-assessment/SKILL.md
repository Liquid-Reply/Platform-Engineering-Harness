# Toil Assessment

Use this skill to turn recurring operational pain into evidence-based improvement candidates rather than automating anecdotes.

## Workflow

1. Define the activity, trigger, performer, frequency, duration, variance, failure rate, and affected teams. Label estimates and their source.
2. Test whether the work is manual, repetitive, automatable, tactical, low enduring value, and grows with service demand. Do not require every trait, but explain the judgment.
3. Calculate a baseline when data permits: monthly hours, interruption count, rework/failure cost, and demand trend. Keep formulas and assumptions visible.
4. Identify whether elimination, simplification, self-service, automation, or documentation is the best intervention—in that order of consideration.
5. Compare candidates using expected time saved, risk reduction, implementation/maintenance cost, reach, confidence, and accountable owner.
6. Define a thin experiment with success metrics, guardrails, rollback, and an observation period. Avoid full automation of an unstable or unsafe process.

## Verification

Ensure recommendations trace to measured or explicitly estimated evidence. Include maintenance and exception-handling costs. Define how the post-change baseline will be measured.

## Output

Provide evidence, toil classification, baseline, prioritized options, experiment design, owner questions, and measurable follow-up.
